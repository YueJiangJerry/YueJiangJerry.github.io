Research focus

Materials activated by the sustainable energy already around us.

My research connects materials design with catalytic performance. I study how mechanical vibration, ultrasound, and light can work together to drive useful chemical reactions under milder conditions.

Mechanism framework

From mechanical stimulus to catalytic charge.

A unified view of how strain, acoustic pressure, and friction reshape charge behaviour and enable redox chemistry across energy, biomedical, and environmental applications.

  • Piezo­catalysis
  • Sonocatalysis
  • Tribocatalysis
  • Flexocatalysis
01

Mechanically driven catalysis

Piezo-, sono-, and mechanochemical pathways that convert vibration into charge separation and catalytic activity.

  • Piezo-photocatalysis
  • Ultrasonic activation
  • Ferroelectric materials
02

Green hydrogen

Defect-engineered and heterojunction catalysts for hydrogen generation from water and seawater.

03

Waste-to-value chemistry

Catalytic reforming of plastics, textiles, and biomass into syngas and sustainable chemical feedstocks.

04

Functional oxide design

Composition, defects, interfaces, and high-entropy architectures that tune material behaviour from the atomic scale upward.

05

Environmental remediation

Catalytic destruction and defluorination of PFAS, alongside degradation of persistent organic contaminants in water.
